# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We want participation in this project to be a straightforward experience for
everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability,
ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of
experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance,
race, caste, colour, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that make this a project people can
contribute to without having to be tough about it.

## Expected behaviour

- Assume the other person is trying to get something right.
- Give and accept feedback about **the work**. "This regex will not match a
  multi-line 250 reply" is feedback; "you clearly did not read the RFC" is not,
  even when it is true, and the first one is more useful anyway.
- Say what you did not check. A named gap is a contribution.
- When you are wrong, say so plainly and move on. This project's own documents
  are full of records of it being wrong; that is the standard, not an
  embarrassment.
- Respect that people have different amounts of time and context. A maintainer
  taking two weeks is not ignoring you.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Sexualised language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind.
- Trolling, insults, derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks.
- Public or private harassment.
- Publishing other people's private information — a physical or email address, a
  workplace, an account handle they have not connected to this project —
  without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption: relitigating settled decisions, demanding free labour,
  or bad-faith escalation.
- Other conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional setting.

**One specific to this project, because of what it does.** Do not use this
project's spaces to solicit, coordinate, or brag about scanning networks you are
not authorized to scan, and do not post scan results identifying a third party's
hosts without that party's permission. Issues, discussions and pull requests are
public; a "here is what I found on 203.0.113.0/24" post is somebody else's
network on a public page. Use `lab/`, RFC 5737 documentation ranges, or your own
addresses in examples. This will be treated as harassment of the third party,
and the content will be removed.

## Scope

This applies in every space this project occupies — issues, pull requests,
discussions, commit messages, code comments, and any place where someone is
representing the project — and it applies to conduct outside those spaces when
it is directed at a project participant because of their participation.

## Reporting

Report a concern by **opening a GitHub issue containing only the words "conduct
report, please contact me privately"** and no details. A private channel will be
opened for the details. Do not put the details in a public issue; you should not
have to describe what happened in public in order to report it.

If the concern is about the maintainers, or if you would rather not go through
this repository at all, GitHub's own reporting route is
<https://github.com/contact/report-abuse>, and it is a legitimate first stop
rather than a last resort.

All reports will be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly, and the
reporter's identity will be kept confidential.

**An honest limitation, stated rather than glossed over.** This is a small
project. There is no independent ethics committee here and it would be dishonest
to imply otherwise; enforcement is done by the maintainers, who are the same
people who might be the subject of a report. That is the reason the GitHub route
above is named explicitly and is not framed as an escalation of last resort.

## Enforcement

Maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing these standards, and
will take appropriate and fair corrective action in response to any behaviour
they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive, or harmful. They may remove,
edit, or reject comments, commits, code, issues, and other contributions that do
not align with this Code of Conduct, and will give a reason when they do.

Consequences escalate roughly as follows, and the maintainers may skip steps
where the conduct warrants it:

1. **A private correction** — a written note explaining what was inappropriate
   and why, possibly with a request for a public apology.
2. **A warning**, with consequences for continued behaviour, including no
   interaction with the people involved for a stated period.
3. **A temporary ban** from any interaction with the project.
4. **A permanent ban** from any public interaction with the project.
